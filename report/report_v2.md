# Developing inner light: what the sources tell a practitioner to do

Seventy-five sources were read. Earlier records came mostly from keyword-selected digests; seventeen have since been read in full in their original language, so confidence is now high for about twenty-eight records and medium for most of the rest. Each claim below is labelled by kind: a classical text prescribing, a teacher asserting, or a practitioner reporting.

## 1. What is being claimed

The sources mark light as seen, and not imagined, in three ways.

Some simply deny imagination. Kirpal Singh, answering disciples, says that one sees and cannot visualise, and forbids picturing a face or a candle [snt-kirpal-light-of-kirpal]. Aurobindo tells correspondents that no imagination is involved [hin-aurobindo-letters-on-yoga-3]. Sagan's manual rules out visualisation and counts a faint whitish haze as the real beginning [mod-sagan-awakening-third-eye]. Longchenpa, as translated by Geisshuesler, says the lights of Togal are seen directly through the eyes, without any effort of visualisation [vaj-geisshuesler-sky-gazing].

Others offer a test. Ibn 'Arabi tells the retreatant to close the eyes: if the sight persists it is imagination, and if it vanishes it was perception tied to a place [suf-ibn-arabi-risalat-al-anwar]. Kubra gives the opposite test: close the eyelids and look at what is in front of you; the colours come only after the image-making faculties have tired and gone quiet [suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal]. Mueller, observing himself, separates an imagined outline, unlit and still, from figures that suddenly stand luminous without his prompting [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]. A correspondent printed by Galton contrasts the willed, dim, far-off mental image with the involuntary, close, daylight-clear vision [mod-galton-inquiries-human-faculty].

A third group places the light in or before the eye itself: the Zohar in the shut eye pressed or rolled [jew-zohar-closed-eye-colours], the Zhen'gao in light seen on pressing the eye corners [dao-zhengao-j9-10], Russell in a glow replacing the cloudy gloom behind the lids [wes-ae-candle-of-vision].

The line blurs in several places. Pa-Auk says the white sign is produced by perception and can be reshaped at will [bud-pa-auk-knowing-and-seeing]. Vital says the first phase is plain imagination and that the light afterwards presents itself as though seen [jew-vital-shaarei-kedushah]. Razi calls the lights perceived in the dark cell but says their forms and colours are imagination's clothing [suf-razi-mirsad-al-ibad]. Ibn 'Ata' Allah says plainly that you see lights rising and descending, yet never says whether eye or heart sees [suf-ibn-ata-allah-miftah-al-falah]. The perception-of-light suttas speak of a perception attended to, not of anything described as seen [bud-suttas-perception-of-light]. Bates's palming patients insisted they saw their lights with their eyes; Bates, as their physician, called every one an illusion [mod-bates-perfect-sight].

## 2. The technique families

**Looking into the closed-eye darkness.** Three Sant Mat teachers, in letters and talks, give the same instruction: a posture you can hold, eyes closed, body and breath forgotten, a gaze without strain into the middle of the darkness between and behind the eyebrows, with slow repetition of the given names [snt-kirpal-spiritual-elixir, snt-kirpal-teachings-meditation, snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]. Sawan Singh sets an hour and a half daily [snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]. Reported in order: sparks, steady light, a starry sky, a large star, sun and moon [snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems, snt-kirpal-light-of-kirpal]. Mueller, a practitioner observing himself, sits in a dark place, calm, unfed and without wine, eye muscles slack, thought refused; light-spots and mists come first, then often a dull inner daylight, then luminous images [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]. Galton, in perfect darkness, watched the field with the strain used to make out a sign-post at night [mod-galton-inquiries-human-faculty]. They conflict on strain, which Sant Mat and Mueller reject and Galton uses, and on initiation, which only Sant Mat requires.

**Gazing at a source and keeping what remains.** Stein and Ingram's retreat notes give the fullest procedure: a candle three to five feet away, a minute or two of staring, eyes closed on the after-image until it is gone, repeated many times [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Sivananda lists ten trataka objects, starts at two minutes and holds the image with closed eyes [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga]. The Contemplation Sutra holds the setting sun until it stays with eyes shut or open [bud-contemplation-sutra-takakusu]. Two Indian texts gaze at one's own shadow, then at the sky [hin-rama-prasad-natures-finer-forces, hin-shiva-samhita-vasu].

**Taking a sign from a prepared object.** The Visuddhimagga prescribes, for the light kasina, a circle of sun or moonlight through a hole, or a lamp in a pot facing a wall, viewed with eyes moderately open until a learning sign and then a bright counterpart sign appear [bud-visuddhimagga-nanamoli]. The Vimuttimagga uses water throwing sunlight on a wall [bud-vimuttimagga-ehara]. Pa-Auk holds the white sign for one to three hours [bud-pa-auk-knowing-and-seeing].

**Concentration on something that is not light.** Pa-Auk keeps attention at the breath's touch point, where grey smoke, then white, then a morning-star brightness are said to come [bud-pa-auk-knowing-and-seeing]. The canonical suttas tell a meditator to apply the mind to the perception of light, the same by night as by day, but give no procedure for obtaining it [bud-suttas-perception-of-light]. The Upakkilesa Sutta makes light a gauge of immersion [bud-mn128-upakkilesa-sutta].

**Fixed gaze and posture.** Classical texts prescribe half-closed eyes on the nose tip [hin-hatha-yoga-pradipika-sinh]; open eyes on the nose tip or into space [hin-thirty-minor-upanishads-aiyar]; lids lowered until the nose tip is just visible, set once and let go, with attention resting level between the brows, where the Golden Flower says the light enters of itself [dao-jinhua-zongzhi-chinese]; and Togal's upward, sideways and lowered gazes, pupils still, on a cloudless sky or in a dark room [vaj-baker-tibetan-yoga-togal, vaj-geisshuesler-sky-gazing]. Sagan and Mehi say not to turn the eyeballs toward the brow [mod-sagan-awakening-third-eye, snt-mehi-philosophy-of-liberation].

**Repetition in seclusion.** Ghazali prescribes a solitary corner and unbroken repetition of the divine name [suf-ghazali-ihya-dhikr-seclusion-method]; Ibn 'Arabi adds a closed door and moderate diet [suf-ibn-arabi-risalat-al-anwar]. Three classical Sufi manuals add an induction regime. Razi prescribes forty days in a small dark cell, cross-legged, eyes closed, with the formula received from a shaykh struck from navel to heart [suf-razi-mirsad-al-ibad]. Ibn 'Ata' Allah gives a body-sized cell admitting no daylight, and a sitting with head between the knees and eyes shut to the senses in which, he says, lights come [suf-ibn-ata-allah-miftah-al-falah]. Kubra gives Junayd's eight conditions, among them fasting, silence, seclusion, constant dhikr and the heart bound to the shaykh; he does not himself say the cell is dark [suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal]. First lights are flashes and gleams [suf-razi-mirsad-al-ibad, suf-ghazali-ihya-dhikr-seclusion-method].

**Building an image until it is seen.** The Dhammakaya manual moves an imagined crystal sphere through seven bases until a bright sphere arises [bud-dhammakaya-luang-phor-sodh-manual]. The Zhen'gao has the practitioner, eyes half closed, make the eyes see five-coloured vapours over the face [dao-zhengao-j9-10]. A tulpa guide imposes a glowing sphere in a blacked-out room [frm-tulpa-luigi-tulpamancy-guide]. Kirpal Singh forbids visualising [snt-kirpal-light-of-kirpal], and Mueller found that willed images stay unlit [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen].

**Scrying.** Melville and Sepharial agree: a shaded room, the crystal screened in black, ten to fifteen minutes daily at the same hour; the crystal dulls, shows star-like points, clouds, then clears [wes-melville-crystal-gazing, wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal].

**Sense closure and eye pressure.** Fingers close ears, nose, mouth and eyes in one verse [hin-rama-prasad-natures-finer-forces]. The Zohar says to close and press or roll the eye and shining colours appear, but uses this as a demonstration, not a regimen [jew-zohar-closed-eye-colours]. The Zhen'gao ends an eye massage by pressing the eye corners eighteen times until light is seen distinctly [dao-zhengao-j9-10]. Longchenpa has the Togal beginner press the eyeballs lightly with two fingers [vaj-geisshuesler-sky-gazing]. Bates's patients, palming with no pressure, saw lights at once [mod-bates-perfect-sight].

**Dark room.** Randolph says sparks appear within minutes in a fully dark room [wes-randolph-seership]; Togal names a dark chamber and a forty-nine-day retreat [vaj-geisshuesler-sky-gazing].

**Breath and energy work.** The three Wu-Liu manuals and the Golden Flower treat light as a timing sign: a lightning-like flash between the brows during the firing of essence means continue, and the second and third flashes, after some three hundred circuits and with the genitals retracted, mean stop [dao-dacheng-jieyao, dao-wu-shouyang-xianfo-hezong, dao-huiming-jing-chinese].

**Waiting on gift.** Hilton's light springs little by little in restful darkness and is not bodily [chr-hilton-scale-of-perfection]. Aua's light came in solitude after teaching failed [shm-rasmussen-iglulik-intellectual-culture].

## 3. Breaking through versus developing

**What is offered to someone who sees nothing.** The answers are of six kinds.

*Supply the first light from outside.* A flame after-image gives a seen object at the first sitting. Stein reports a stable red dot within hours and brilliant colour by day nine of twenty-five [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Sivananda, as a teacher, gives trataka the same role, with onset from days to nine months, and adds that some never see lights and still progress [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga]. This remains the answer with the most dated practitioner testimony.

*Keep looking into the dark.* Sawan Singh tells a disciple who sees nothing to keep peering, and the darkness will turn to light [snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]. Russell reports weeks or months of stupor first [wes-ae-candle-of-vision]. Galton is the one self-declared non-seer: he visualised poorly, rarely saw after-images, and believed his dark field uniform black, yet after habituating himself to strained watching in perfect darkness he found a continual shifting of patterns, faint and fugitive, which he says many will find [mod-galton-inquiries-human-faculty]. Mueller holds that everyone has traces and that those who lack them by day can find them before sleep [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen].

*Recognise what is already there.* Sagan asserts that most who think they see nothing overlook a faint haze [mod-sagan-awakening-third-eye]. Kubra says the same in classical form: one who closes the eyes and sees nothing is in fact seeing the darkness of his own existence, which is the first stage [suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal].

*Thin the self by regime.* Kubra's remedy for that darkness is striving: less food, surrender to a shaykh, the eight conditions [suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal]. Ibn 'Ata' Allah says to stay on the shaykh's formula until its fruit appears, waking or in dream, without haste [suf-ibn-ata-allah-miftah-al-falah]. Razi grades the lights by the heart's polish [suf-razi-mirsad-al-ibad]. The Zhen'gao concedes that at first there is not even a semblance, advises beginning with one direction, and counts results in years; one adept saw nothing in forty years until his illness was treated [dao-zhengao-j9-10].

*Repeat harder.* The Dhammakaya manual says to keep imagining and reciting a hundred or a thousand times [bud-dhammakaya-luang-phor-sodh-manual]; the crystal manuals prescribe the same short sitting for months [wes-melville-crystal-gazing, wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal].

*Borrow someone else's attention.* Tawakkul Beg saw little until his master intervened in person [suf-tawakkul-beg-initiation-by-mulla-shah].

**Negative testimony.** One ex-member of the Divine Light Mission was promised a light brighter than a thousand suns, saw none at initiation, and reports none across twenty-five years of daily practice; failure was explained to him as his own impurity [snt-expremie-journey-ajw]. His techniques are not described, so the account shows only that transmission plus diligence did not suffice for him.

**Sources that only develop.** The Wu-Liu manuals uniformly refuse induction: light comes only after the work, cannot be hurried, and light without the bodily sign is delusion [dao-dacheng-jieyao, dao-wu-shouyang-xianfo-hezong, dao-huiming-jing-chinese]. The Golden Flower says not to care whether there is light or not [dao-jinhua-zongzhi-chinese]. The Upakkilesa Sutta troubleshoots light already present [bud-mn128-upakkilesa-sutta]; Mahasi treats it as a by-product [bud-mahasi-progress-of-insight].

**Sources that say it cannot be forced.** Teresa is flat: no diligence obtains it [chr-teresa-life]. Ghazali leaves only waiting after repetition [suf-ghazali-ihya-dhikr-seclusion-method]. Al-Sanusi, via Rinn, says the lights are subject to God's will and most affiliates never see them [suf-rinn-marabouts-et-khouan-1884]. Mueller, despite half-days of practice in the dark, could almost never summon a chosen luminous image; they came suddenly, against his will [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]. The Visuddhimagga puts success at one in a hundred or a thousand [bud-visuddhimagga-nanamoli]; Sepharial says failure after earnest trial means the faculty is absent [wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal]. Galton calls the visionary tendency hereditary and unequal, though blunted by repression and sharpened by dwelling on it [mod-galton-inquiries-human-faculty].

## 4. Physical induction and stimulus independence

**Claims that the light outlasts the stimulus.** Newton, after brief sun exposures, found attention alone recalled the image in the dark, more easily each time [mod-newton-letter-sun-phantasm]. Stein and Ingram describe colour organising out of the murk after the dot has gone, though an LED refresh is still used [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. The Contemplation Sutra and Visuddhimagga require the image to stand free of its source [bud-contemplation-sutra-takakusu, bud-visuddhimagga-nanamoli]. The one classical claim for pressure comes from Longchenpa: press the eyeballs lightly for the first three or four days, after which appearances arise without pressure [vaj-geisshuesler-sky-gazing]. That record is pass_flagged on provenance: it is an academic's fragmentary translation chosen for a historical argument, and the daytime practice keeps the sky as a visual ground. It shows a tradition making the claim; it does not show anyone achieving it.

**Reports that it does not.** The only practitioner report on pressure runs the other way. Strasburg pressed his eyes daily for two years with intense light; when the instruction changed to steadying without pressure, almost all of it went [snt-expremie-journey-strasburg]. Aurobindo says pressure cannot force colour [hin-aurobindo-letters-on-yoga-3]. The Zhen'gao's pressure light may mean light seen while pressing or clearly afterwards; the text cannot settle it [dao-zhengao-j9-10]. The Zohar ties its colours to the eye's own colours and says they cannot be held [jew-zohar-closed-eye-colours]. Scrying light stays in the crystal [wes-melville-crystal-gazing, wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal].

**The inverse case.** Bates wanted a perfect black and treated light as failure. His patients, eyes merely covered, saw lights at once; fatigue, hunger, worry and above all effort to suppress them made them stronger, while relaxed memory of a small black point quenched them [mod-bates-perfect-sight]. Read in reverse, darkness yields light readily and strain feeds it, which sides with Galton against Mueller and Sant Mat on strain.

## 5. Once light appears

Advice divides into holding still and going through. Pa-Auk says stay at the touch point until the sign is stable, then rest on it for hours [bud-pa-auk-knowing-and-seeing]. The Upakkilesa Sutta lists states that make light vanish, among them doubt, dullness, elation and energy too tight or slack [bud-mn128-upakkilesa-sutta]. Mueller reports that eye movement or reflection scatters the images [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]; Galton, that any other thought abolishes them [mod-galton-inquiries-human-faculty]. Longchenpa says to keep eyes and mind unmoving and hold the restless chains inside the blue expanse until they steady [vaj-geisshuesler-sky-gazing]. Kubra reads colours as a gauge, steady green meaning a settled heart, and tests each fire by the state that comes with it [suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal]. Ibn 'Ata' Allah says to report everything to the shaykh and not to turn and look at the dhikr itself [suf-ibn-ata-allah-miftah-al-falah]. The Sant Mat teachers say to look into the middle of the light until it bursts [snt-kirpal-spiritual-elixir, snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]; Ibn 'Arabi, not to stop at any unveiling [suf-ibn-arabi-risalat-al-anwar].

## 6. Dissent and cautions

Several traditions say to leave the light alone. Mahasi calls it a corruption of insight [bud-mahasi-progress-of-insight]. The Cloud author calls lights sought by straining a deceit, and John of the Cross says to take no pleasure in visions [chr-cloud-of-unknowing, chr-john-of-the-cross-ascent]. Faqir Chand treats forms in the light as projection [snt-faqir-chand-unknowing-sage]. The Golden Flower says light seized as light becomes consciousness-light [dao-jinhua-zongzhi-chinese]; the Huiming jing calls visions of buddhas demonic [dao-huiming-jing-chinese]. Razi treats every form and colour as a veil [suf-razi-mirsad-al-ibad]; Sha'rani, quoted by Rinn, calls lights from fasting and sleeplessness delusions [suf-rinn-marabouts-et-khouan-1884]. Abulafia's recitation, the one Jewish procedure read in full, promises joy and apprehension and names no seen light [jew-abulafia-or-ha-sekhel]. Galton and Mueller, as observers, deny the images any objective or religious standing [mod-galton-inquiries-human-faculty, mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen].

Safety, plainly. Sun: Newton lost the use of his eyes for days [mod-newton-letter-sun-phantasm]; Bates reports patches lasting months yet recommends sun-gazing, which should not be followed [mod-bates-perfect-sight]. Eyes: Mehi warns that pressing causes pain and abnormality [snt-mehi-philosophy-of-liberation]; Longchenpa softens an older instruction to press firmly into a slight press [vaj-geisshuesler-sky-gazing]. Breath: Teresa says holding it harms prayer [chr-teresa-interior-castle]. Darkness, fasting and vigil: Ibn 'Ata' Allah warns that excess dryness in retreat brings imaginings and raving [suf-ibn-ata-allah-miftah-al-falah]; Kubra's shaykh feared madness or death at the stage of head pains [suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal]; Stein and Ingram advise a guide through the fear stage [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Effort: a Bates patient who strained to suppress her lights went into convulsions [mod-bates-perfect-sight].

## 7. Where to start

**Candle after-image.** Sit three to five feet from a flame; look for a minute or two; close the eyes and follow the after-image until nothing organised remains; repeat [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Begin short [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga]. As it fades, attend to it as to something hard to see [mod-newton-letter-sun-phantasm]. Well supported: that something is seen at once. Thin: independence from the flame. Never use the sun.

**Closed-eye darkness.** Daily, in a dark place, calm and unfed, eyes closed and unpressed, look into the middle of the dark [snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems, mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]. Count any haze as the beginning [mod-sagan-awakening-third-eye, suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal]. If nothing comes, try Galton's deciphering attention, keeping all other thought out [mod-galton-inquiries-human-faculty], or watch at the edge of sleep [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]. Well supported: that a non-seer can find faint patterns. Unresolved: strain versus slackness.

**Lowered-lid gaze.** Lower the lids until the nose tip is just visible, rest attention between the brows, listen to the breath until inaudible, and do not care whether light comes, for a hundred days [dao-jinhua-zongzhi-chinese]. Classical prescription only.

**Dhikr in a dark cell.** Only under a shaykh who gives the formula: closed eyes, a cell without daylight, lightened diet, the same formula until its fruit appears [suf-razi-mirsad-al-ibad, suf-ibn-ata-allah-miftah-al-falah, suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal]. Well specified by three classical texts; no practitioner report beyond the authors; not for solitary use.

## 8. Gaps and confidence

Tibetan practice now rests on three records, one of them pass_flagged, and no dark-retreat text has been read. Pressure-free continuation after eye pressure is claimed classically and contradicted by the only practitioner report. Jewish practice has one procedure, which yields no reported light. Taoist alchemy refuses induction; the Zhen'gao's sun and moon methods remain partly unread. The Sufi induction regime has no modern first-person witness. Galton is the only non-seer who succeeded, and his gain was faint patterns, not steady light.
