# Developing inner light: what the sources tell a practitioner to do

Sixty-seven sources were read, most from keyword-selected digests, so the typical record carries medium confidence. Each claim below is labelled by kind: a classical text prescribing, a teacher asserting, or a practitioner reporting.

## 1. What is being claimed

The sources mark light as seen, and not imagined, in three ways.

Some simply deny imagination. Kirpal Singh, answering disciples, says that one sees and cannot visualise, and forbids picturing a face or a candle [snt-kirpal-light-of-kirpal]. Aurobindo tells correspondents that no imagination is involved and that whatever is seen with closed eyes can later be seen with open ones [hin-aurobindo-letters-on-yoga-3]. Sagan's manual rules out visualisation and counts a faint whitish haze as the real beginning [mod-sagan-awakening-third-eye]. The Kalacakra verses say the signs are seen without having been imagined, with the eyes neither closed nor open [vaj-sekoddesa-summary-of-empowerment-84000].

Others offer a test. Ibn 'Arabi instructs the retreatant to close the eyes: if the sight persists it is imagination, and if it vanishes it was perception tied to a place [suf-ibn-arabi-risalat-al-anwar]. Teresa reports that the light was the same with eyes open or shut and that no effort of hers could picture it [chr-teresa-life]. Mueller, observing himself, separates images that are luminous in the dark field from shapes merely thought there [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen].

A third group places the light in or before the eye itself: the Zohar in the pressed or rolled eyeball [jew-zohar-closed-eye-colours], the Togal authors quoted by Baker in phenomena native to the eye [vaj-baker-tibetan-yoga-togal], and Russell in a glow replacing the ordinary cloudy gloom behind the lids [wes-ae-candle-of-vision].

The line blurs most in Theravada and in the visualising traditions. Pa-Auk describes white wherever one looks, yet says the sign is produced by perception and can be reshaped at will [bud-pa-auk-knowing-and-seeing]. The Vimuttimagga says the after-image is seen with closed eyes but exists only in thought [bud-vimuttimagga-ehara]. Vital states that the first phase is plain imagination and that the light afterwards presents itself as though seen [jew-vital-shaarei-kedushah]; the Dhammakaya manual starts from an imagined sphere and insists the aim is to see it [bud-dhammakaya-luang-phor-sodh-manual]. Teresa and Hilton place their light in interior sight and treat bodily lights as another matter [chr-teresa-interior-castle, chr-hilton-scale-of-perfection]. Sivananda himself allows that a vision may be subjective, objective or imaginary [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga].

## 2. The technique families

**Looking into the closed-eye darkness.** Three Sant Mat teachers, in letters and talks, give the same instruction: take the posture you can hold longest, close the eyes, forget body and breath, and look without strain into the middle of the darkness between and behind the eyebrows, repeating the given names slowly so that the gaze is not disturbed [snt-kirpal-spiritual-elixir, snt-kirpal-teachings-meditation, snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]. Sawan Singh sets an hour and a half of repetition daily; Kirpal Singh favours early morning [snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems, snt-kirpal-spiritual-elixir]. Reported in order: sparks and flashes, steady light, a starry sky, a large star, sun and moon [snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems, snt-kirpal-light-of-kirpal]. Outside the lineage, Mueller sits in a very dark place, calm, eyes still, not after food, and reports the field brightening into a dim inner daylight [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]. They converge on not straining. They conflict on initiation, which Sant Mat requires and the others do not mention.

**Gazing at a source and keeping what remains.** Stein and Ingram's retreat notes give the fullest procedure: a candle three to five feet away, a minute or two of staring, eyes closed on the after-image until it is gone, repeated many times [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Sivananda lists ten trataka objects, starts at two minutes, gazes until tears and then holds the image with closed eyes; a star or moon watched from a terrace is said to yield coloured lights and a mass of light with the eyes open [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga]. The Contemplation Sutra begins with the setting sun, held until it stays whether the eyes are shut or open [bud-contemplation-sutra-takakusu]. Two Indian texts give a shadow method: gaze at the neck of your own shadow, then look into the sky [hin-rama-prasad-natures-finer-forces, hin-shiva-samhita-vasu].

**Taking a sign from a prepared object.** The Visuddhimagga prescribes, for the light kasina, a circle of sun or moonlight through a hole, or a lamp in a pot facing a wall, viewed with eyes moderately open while its name is repeated, until a learning sign and then a compact bright counterpart sign appear [bud-visuddhimagga-nanamoli]. The Vimuttimagga uses a bowl of water throwing sunlight on a wall and forbids scanning [bud-vimuttimagga-ehara]. Pa-Auk has the yogi look, close the eyes and visualise, with the light of breath concentration as a fallback, then hold the white sign for one to three hours [bud-pa-auk-knowing-and-seeing].

**Concentration on something that is not light.** Pa-Auk keeps attention at the touch point of the breath; grey smoke, then cotton-wool white, then a morning-star brightness are said to come there [bud-pa-auk-knowing-and-seeing]. The Upakkilesa Sutta treats light as a gauge: its extent follows the extent of immersion [bud-mn128-upakkilesa-sutta]. Aurobindo locates the opening at the brow centre and says a quiet mind is not required [hin-aurobindo-letters-on-yoga-3].

**Fixed gaze and posture.** Classical texts prescribe half-closed eyes on the nose tip with the breath stilled [hin-hatha-yoga-pradipika-sinh]; fully open eyes aimed at the nose tip, yielding darkness and then a sphere of light, or a gaze into space where colours appear at set distances [hin-thirty-minor-upanishads-aiyar]; lids lowered until the nose tip is just visible, attention resting between the brows [dao-jinhua-zongzhi-chinese]; and three Togal postures with upward, sideways and lowered gazes, held motionless in darkness or open light [vaj-baker-tibetan-yoga-togal]. Against this, Sagan and Mehi say not to turn the eyeballs toward the brow [mod-sagan-awakening-third-eye, snt-mehi-philosophy-of-liberation].

**Repetition in seclusion.** Ghazali prescribes a solitary corner and unbroken repetition of the divine name until the tongue stops and the word's form leaves the heart [suf-ghazali-ihya-dhikr-seclusion-method]. Ibn 'Arabi adds a closed door and a moderate diet [suf-ibn-arabi-risalat-al-anwar]; Suhrawardi, forty days of little food and vigil [suf-suhrawardi-hikmat-al-ishraq-lights]. The lights reported begin as lightning-like flashes [suf-ghazali-ihya-dhikr-seclusion-method, suf-suhrawardi-hikmat-al-ishraq-lights].

**Building an image until it is seen.** The Dhammakaya manual moves an imagined crystal sphere through seven bases with a recited formula until a bright sphere arises [bud-dhammakaya-luang-phor-sodh-manual]. Tawakkul Beg reproduces his master's image with bandaged eyes [suf-tawakkul-beg-initiation-by-mulla-shah]. A tulpa community guide imposes a glowing sphere in a blacked-out room [frm-tulpa-luigi-tulpamancy-guide]. Kirpal Singh forbids visualising and Sawan Singh calls a contemplated photograph a reflection of one's own mind [snt-kirpal-light-of-kirpal, snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems].

**Scrying.** Melville and Sepharial, both teacher manuals, agree closely: a shaded room, the crystal screened in black, ten to fifteen minutes daily at the same hour; the crystal dulls, shows pin-points like stars, clouds, then clears to a blue field [wes-melville-crystal-gazing, wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal].

**Sense closure and eye pressure.** A classical verse has the fingers stop ears, nose, mouth and eyes [hin-rama-prasad-natures-finer-forces]; another closes the ears, attends to the sound and expects blue light [hin-thirty-minor-upanishads-aiyar]; the Zohar says to press or roll the closed eye [jew-zohar-closed-eye-colours].

**Dark room.** Randolph says sparks or phosphor clouds appear within minutes in a fully dark room and that repetition develops them [wes-randolph-seership].

**Breath and energy work.** Three Wu-Liu alchemical manuals treat flashes between the brows as a signal after some three hundred cycles of circulation, counted as three appearances [dao-dacheng-jieyao, dao-wu-shouyang-xianfo-hezong, dao-huiming-jing-chinese].

**Waiting on gift.** Hilton tells the contemplative to stay in a restful darkness until light springs little by little, but that light is expressly not bodily [chr-hilton-scale-of-perfection]. Aua, an Iglulik shaman, reports that the light came in solitude after teaching had failed [shm-rasmussen-iglulik-intellectual-culture].

## 3. Breaking through versus developing

**What is offered to someone who sees nothing.** The answers are of five kinds.

*Supply the first light from outside.* A flame after-image gives a seen object at the first sitting. Stein reports a stable red dot within hours and brilliant colour by day nine of twenty-five, and Ingram reports later doing the practice cold, from eyelid colours alone [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Sivananda, asserting as a teacher, gives trataka the same role, with onset anywhere from a few days to six or nine months; he adds that some never see lights and still progress [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga]. This is the most specific answer in the library and the only one with dated practitioner testimony.

*Keep looking into the dark.* Sawan Singh tells a disciple who sees nothing to keep peering into the darkness, which will turn to light; limb numbness shows attention withdrawing, and sparks come when concentration is nearly complete [snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]. Kirpal Singh compares it to climbing a dark staircase with glimpses near the top [snt-kirpal-light-of-kirpal]. The Taiping text calls darkness behind closed eyes the normal start and puts first signs at about a hundred days [dao-taiping-shengjun-mizhi]. Russell reports weeks or months of stupor first, and says he knew people without natural vision who succeeded [wes-ae-candle-of-vision]. These agree, but the Sant Mat version is tied to initiation, at which the teachers say a first experience is given [snt-kirpal-light-of-kirpal, snt-kirpal-teachings-meditation].

*Recognise what is already there.* Sagan asserts that most people who think they see nothing are overlooking a faint haze, which can be amplified [mod-sagan-awakening-third-eye]. The Golden Flower calls the first hundred days' light mere eye-light, with true light following [dao-jinhua-zongzhi-chinese].

*Repeat harder.* The Dhammakaya manual has a section for one who has not seen the sphere: keep imagining and reciting at the body centre, a hundred or a thousand times [bud-dhammakaya-luang-phor-sodh-manual]. The crystal manuals prescribe the same short sitting for days, weeks or months [wes-melville-crystal-gazing, wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal].

*Borrow someone else's attention.* Tawakkul Beg saw nothing for three nights, a little colourless light on the fourth, and the full opening only when his master intervened in person [suf-tawakkul-beg-initiation-by-mulla-shah].

**Sources that only develop.** The Upakkilesa Sutta troubleshoots light that already comes and goes [bud-mn128-upakkilesa-sutta]. Mahasi treats light as a by-product of noticing [bud-mahasi-progress-of-insight]. The alchemical manuals say light arrives after the prior work and that earlier light is delusion [dao-dacheng-jieyao, dao-huiming-jing-chinese]. The Sufi colour schemes describe lights once present [suf-fleischer-farbige-lichterscheinungen-1862, suf-rinn-marabouts-et-khouan-1884]. Mueller had his images from youth and gives conditions, not a way in [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen].

**Sources that say it cannot be forced.** Teresa is flat: no diligence obtains it [chr-teresa-life]. Ghazali divides the work: repetition is yours to do, and after it one can only wait [suf-ghazali-ihya-dhikr-seclusion-method]. Kirpal Singh says to sit at the door without clutching, and Sawan Singh says not to look for light at all [snt-kirpal-teachings-meditation, snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]. Aua's light came through solitude, grief and sudden joy [shm-rasmussen-iglulik-intellectual-culture]. Several sources limit who can succeed. The Visuddhimagga puts success at one in a hundred or a thousand [bud-visuddhimagga-nanamoli]; Aurobindo confines effort to those with some inborn faculty [hin-aurobindo-letters-on-yoga-3]; Sepharial says failure after earnest trial means the faculty is absent [wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal]. On whether strong visualisers do better, Goodrich-Freer says yes and Thomas says often not [wes-goodrich-freer-essays, wes-thomas-crystal-gazing].

## 4. Physical induction and stimulus independence

**Claims that the light outlasts the stimulus.** Newton's report is the clearest. After three brief exposures he turned his attention on the fading image as one strains to see something faint, and it returned at full strength; thereafter attention alone recalled it in the dark, more easily each time, and it appeared in the unexposed eye [mod-newton-letter-sun-phantasm]. Stein and Ingram describe the same hinge: stay with what remains after the dot has gone, and colour and form organise out of the murk, later carried into eyes-open walking; an LED refresh is still used, so independence is partial [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Wenger, a teacher, claims that describing an after-image aloud prolongs it for minutes [mod-wenger-image-streaming-backup]. Among classical texts, the Contemplation Sutra requires the sun's image to persist with eyes shut or open [bud-contemplation-sutra-takakusu], the Visuddhimagga's counterpart sign stands free of the disc [bud-visuddhimagga-nanamoli], and the Siva Samhita says the sky figure is at last seen in the heart [hin-shiva-samhita-vasu]. In each case the stated mechanism is attention kept on the remainder.

**Reports that it does not.** Strasburg pressed his eyes daily for two years with intense light; when the instruction changed to steadying without pressure, almost all of it went [snt-expremie-journey-strasburg]. Aurobindo says physical pressure cannot force colour [hin-aurobindo-letters-on-yoga-3]. Scrying light stays in the crystal in both manuals [wes-melville-crystal-gazing, wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal]. For breath, sound and movement the library holds prescriptions but no report either way [hin-thirty-minor-upanishads-aiyar, wes-randolph-seership, wes-mead-mithriac-ritual].

## 5. Once light appears

Advice divides into holding still and going through. Pa-Auk says not to play with the sign or shift to it early: stay at the touch point until it is stable, then rest on it for hours [bud-pa-auk-knowing-and-seeing]. The Upakkilesa Sutta lists eleven states that make light vanish, among them doubt, dullness, elation, terror and energy too tight or too slack [bud-mn128-upakkilesa-sutta]. Sivananda says to sit motionless and breathe slowly [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga]; Mueller reports that the least eye movement or reflection scatters the images [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]; Sepharial warns that a start of joy or fear ends the vision [wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal]. The Sant Mat teachers say to look into the middle of the light, star, sun or moon until it bursts, and to pass through [snt-kirpal-spiritual-elixir, snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]. Ibn 'Arabi says not to stop at any unveiling and to keep repeating through the frightening spark-throwing light [suf-ibn-arabi-risalat-al-anwar].

## 6. Dissent and cautions

Several traditions say to leave the light alone. Mahasi calls it a corruption of insight, to be recognised as not the path [bud-mahasi-progress-of-insight]. An anonymous Sufi manual advises turning away from coloured lights, which belong to the human subject [suf-fleischer-farbige-lichterscheinungen-1862]. The Cloud author calls lights seen by those who strain inward a deceit, and John of the Cross says to take no pleasure in visions [chr-cloud-of-unknowing, chr-john-of-the-cross-ascent]. Faqir Chand, after seventy years in Sant Mat, treats forms in the light as one's own projection [snt-faqir-chand-unknowing-sage]. The Golden Flower says light attended to as light becomes mere consciousness-light [dao-jinhua-zongzhi-chinese]. Aurobindo discourages flame gazing as an opening to passive states [hin-aurobindo-letters-on-yoga-3].

Safety, plainly. Sun: Newton lost the use of his eyes for days and had recurrences for months [mod-newton-letter-sun-phantasm]; Sivananda permits it only under a guide [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga]. Eyes: Mehi warns that straining or pressing causes pain and abnormality [snt-mehi-philosophy-of-liberation]. Breath: Teresa says holding it harms prayer [chr-teresa-interior-castle]; Randolph admits his forced breathing leads toward coma [wes-randolph-seership]. Darkness and retreat: Stein and Ingram describe a fear stage and advise a guide [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Fasting and vigil: Ibn 'Arabi keeps diet moderate because dryness breeds fancies, and Ignatius cut his vigils when unsure of his night illuminations [suf-ibn-arabi-risalat-al-anwar, chr-ignatius-autobiography].

## 7. Where to start

**Candle after-image.** Sit three to five feet from a steady flame in a dim room; look for a minute or two; close the eyes and follow the after-image until nothing organised remains, then give the murk another minute or two; repeat [bud-fire-kasina-book-stein-ingram]. Begin with short sessions and lengthen cautiously [hin-sivananda-kundalini-yoga]. As the image fades, turn attention on it as on something hard to see [mod-newton-letter-sun-phantasm]. Well supported: that something is seen at once, and the order of dot, murk and colour. Thinner: full independence from the flame, which rests on two practitioners. Never use the sun.

**Closed-eye darkness.** Daily, early, in a posture you can hold, with eyes closed and unpressed, look gently into the middle of the dark, ignoring body and breath [snt-kirpal-teachings-meditation, snt-sawan-singh-spiritual-gems]. Choose a very dark place and a calm, unfed state [mod-mueller-phantastische-gesichtserscheinungen]. Count any haze or drifting points as the beginning [mod-sagan-awakening-third-eye]. Expect weeks or months of nothing [wes-ae-candle-of-vision]. Well supported: the instruction itself. Thin: whether it works without initiation, for which there is only Russell's report and Sagan's assertion.

**Lowered-lid gaze.** Lower the lids until the nose tip is just visible, rest attention between the brows, listen to the breath until it is inaudible, and continue for a hundred days [dao-jinhua-zongzhi-chinese]; the yoga texts give a nearby nose-tip gaze [hin-hatha-yoga-pradipika-sinh, hin-thirty-minor-upanishads-aiyar]. Thin throughout: classical prescriptions only, with no practitioner report in the library.

**Shaded-room surface gazing.** Ten to fifteen minutes daily at the same hour, crystal screened in black [wes-melville-crystal-gazing, wes-sepharial-how-to-read-the-crystal]. Well specified, with a detailed sequence of signs; nothing shows the light leaving the crystal.

## 8. Gaps and confidence

Twelve records are high confidence; the rest rest on digests or partial texts. Tibetan practice depends on one secondary chapter and one set of root verses, and dark retreat is unread. Two Sufi records central to the colour tradition are low confidence and are not used above [suf-kubra-fawaih-al-jamal, suf-razi-mirsad-al-ibad]. Sufi seclusion has no instruction for the retreatant who sees nothing. No source documents a person who saw nothing, used pressure, breath or sound, and kept the light after stopping.
