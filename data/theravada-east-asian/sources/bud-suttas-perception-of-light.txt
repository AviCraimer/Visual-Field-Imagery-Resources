===== AN 4.41 Samadhibhavanasutta (Ways of Developing Immersion Further) =====
[0.1] Numbered Discourses 4.41
[0.2] 5. With Rohitassa
[0.3] Ways of Developing Immersion Further
[1.1] “Mendicants, there are these four ways of developing immersion further.
[1.2] What four?
[1.3] There is a way of developing immersion further that leads to blissful meditation in this life.
[1.4] There is a way of developing immersion further that leads to gaining knowledge and vision.
[1.5] There is a way of developing immersion further that leads to mindfulness and awareness.
[1.6] There is a way of developing immersion further that leads to the ending of defilements.
[2.1] And what is the way of developing immersion further that leads to blissful meditation in this life?
[2.2] It’s when a mendicant, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unskillful qualities, enters and remains in the first absorption … second absorption … third absorption … fourth absorption.
[2.3] This is the way of developing immersion further that leads to blissful meditation in this life.
[3.1] And what is the way of developing immersion further that leads to gaining knowledge and vision?
[3.2] It’s when a mendicant applies their mind to the perception of light, focusing on the perception of day:
[3.3] as by day, so by night; as by night, so by day.
[3.4] And so, with an open and unenveloped heart, they develop a mind that’s full of radiance.
[3.5] This is the way of developing immersion further that leads to gaining knowledge and vision.
[4.1] And what is the way of developing immersion further that leads to mindfulness and awareness?
[4.2] It’s when a mendicant knows feelings as they arise, as they remain, and as they go away.
[4.3] They know perceptions as they arise, as they remain, and as they go away. They know thoughts as they arise, as they remain, and as they go away.
[4.4] This is the way of developing immersion further that leads to mindfulness and awareness.
[5.1] And what is the way of developing immersion further that leads to the ending of defilements?
[5.2] It’s when a mendicant meditates observing rise and fall in the five grasping aggregates.
[5.3] ‘Such is form, such is the origin of form, such is the disappearance of form.
[5.4] Such is feeling, such is the origin of feeling, such is the disappearance of feeling.
[5.5] Such is perception, such is the origin of perception, such is the disappearance of perception.
[5.6] Such are choices, such is the origin of choices, such is the disappearance of choices.
[5.7] Such is consciousness, such is the origin of consciousness, such is the disappearance of consciousness.’
[5.8] This is the way of developing immersion further that leads to the ending of defilements.
[5.9] These are the four ways of developing immersion further.
[5.10] And it was in this connection that I said in ‘The Way to the Far Shore’, in ‘The Questions of Puṇṇaka’:
[6.1] ‘Having appraised the world high and low,
[6.2] there is nothing in the world that disturbs them.
[6.3] Peaceful, unclouded, untroubled, <j>with no need for hope,
[6.4] they’ve crossed over rebirth and old age, I declare.’”

===== AN 7.61 Pacalayamanasutta (Nodding Off) =====
[0.1] Numbered Discourses 7.61
[0.2] 6. The Undeclared Points
[0.3] Nodding Off
[1.1] So I have heard.
[1.2] At one time the Buddha was staying in the land of the Bhaggas at Crocodile’s Bellow, in the deer park at Bhesakaḷā’s Wood.
[1.3] Now at that time, in the land of the Magadhans near Kallavāḷamutta Village, Venerable Mahāmoggallāna was nodding off while meditating.
[1.4] The Buddha saw him with his clairvoyance that is purified and superhuman.
[1.6] Then, as easily as a strong person would extend or contract their arm, he vanished from the deer park at Bhesakaḷā’s Wood in the land of the Bhaggas and reappeared in front of Mahāmoggallāna near Kallavāḷamutta Village in the land of the Magadhans.
[1.7] He sat down on the seat spread out
[1.8] and said to Mahāmoggallāna,
[2.1] “Are you nodding off, Moggallāna? Are you nodding off?”
[2.2] “Yes, sir.”
[2.3] “So, Moggallāna, don’t focus on or cultivate the perception that you were meditating on when you fell drowsy.
[2.4] It’s possible that you’ll give up drowsiness in this way.
[3.1] But what if that doesn’t work? Then think about and consider the teaching as you’ve learned and memorized it, examining it with your mind.
[3.2] It’s possible that you’ll give up drowsiness in this way.
[4.1] But what if that doesn’t work? Then rehearse in detail the teaching as you’ve learned and memorized it.
[4.2] It’s possible that you’ll give up drowsiness in this way.
[5.1] But what if that doesn’t work? Then pinch your ears and rub your limbs.
[5.2] It’s possible that you’ll give up drowsiness in this way.
[6.1] But what if that doesn’t work? Then get up from your seat, flush your eyes with water, look around in every direction, and look up at the stars and constellations.
[6.2] It’s possible that you’ll give up drowsiness in this way.
[7.1] But what if that doesn’t work? Then apply your mind to the perception of light, focusing on the perception of day:
[7.2] as by day, so by night; as by night, so by day.
[7.3] And so, with an open and unenveloped heart, develop a mind that’s full of radiance.
[7.4] It’s possible that you’ll give up drowsiness in this way.
[8.1] But what if that doesn’t work? Then walk mindfully, focusing on the perception of before and behind, your faculties directed inwards and your mind not scattered outside.
[8.2] It’s possible that you’ll give up drowsiness in this way.
[9.1] But what if that doesn’t work? Then lie down in the lion’s posture—on the right side, placing one foot on top of the other—mindful and aware, and focused on the time of getting up.
[9.2] When you wake, you should get up quickly, thinking:
[9.3] ‘I will not live indulging in the pleasures of sleeping, lying down, and drowsing.’
[9.4] That’s how you should train.
[10.1] So you should train like this:
[10.2] ‘I will not approach families with my head swollen with vanity.’
[10.3] That’s how you should train.
[10.4] What happens if a mendicant approaches families with a head swollen with vanity? Well, families have business to attend to,
[10.5] so people might not notice when a mendicant arrives. In that case the mendicant thinks:
[10.6] ‘Who on earth has turned this family against me? It seems they don’t like me any more.’
[10.7] And so, because they don’t get anything they feel dismayed. Being dismayed, they become restless. Being restless, they lose restraint. And without restraint the mind is far from immersion.
[11.1] So you should train like this:
[11.2] ‘I won’t get into arguments.’
[11.3] That’s how you should train.
[11.4] When there’s an argument, you can expect there’ll be lots of talking. When there’s lots of talking, people become restless. Being restless, they lose restraint. And without restraint the mind is far from immersion.
[11.5] Moggallāna, I don’t praise all kinds of closeness.
[11.6] Nor do I criticize all kinds of closeness.
[11.7] I don’t praise closeness with laypeople and renunciates.
[11.8] I do praise closeness with those lodgings that are quiet and still, far from the madding crowd, remote from human settlements, and fit for retreat.”
[12.1] When he said this, Venerable Moggallāna asked the Buddha,
[12.2] “Sir, how do you briefly define a mendicant who is freed through the ending of craving, who has reached the ultimate end, the ultimate sanctuary from the yoke, the ultimate spiritual life, the ultimate goal, and is best among gods and humans?”
[13.1] “Take a mendicant who has heard:
[13.2] ‘Nothing is worth insisting on.’
[13.3] When a mendicant has heard that
[13.4] nothing is worth insisting on,
[13.5] they directly know all things. Directly knowing all things, they completely understand all things. Completely understanding all things, when they experience any kind of feeling—pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral—
[13.6] they meditate observing impermanence, dispassion, cessation, and letting go in those feelings.
[13.7] Meditating in this way, they don’t grasp at anything in the world.
[13.8] Not grasping, they’re not anxious. Not being anxious, they personally become extinguished.
[13.9] They understand: ‘Rebirth is ended, the spiritual journey has been completed, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing further for this place.’
[13.10] That’s how I briefly define a mendicant who is freed through the ending of craving, who has reached the ultimate end, the ultimate sanctuary from the yoke, the ultimate spiritual life, the ultimate goal, and is best among gods and humans.”

===== SN 51.20 Vibhangasutta (Analysis, of the bases of psychic power) =====

[2.17] And so, with an open and unenveloped heart, they develop a mind that’s full of radiance.

[5.3] This is called enthusiasm constricted internally.
[6.1] And what is enthusiasm that’s scattered externally?
[6.2] It’s when enthusiasm is frequently scattered and diffused externally on account of the five kinds of sensual stimulation.
[6.3] This is called enthusiasm scattered externally.
[7.1] And how does a mendicant meditate perceiving before and behind:
[7.2] as before, so behind; as behind, so before?
[7.3] It’s when the perception of before and behind is properly grasped, focused on, borne in mind, and penetrated with wisdom by a mendicant.
[7.4] That’s how a mendicant meditates perceiving before and behind:
[7.5] as before, so behind; as behind, so before.
[8.1] And how does a mendicant meditate as below, so above; as above, so below?

[8.4] That’s how a mendicant meditates as below, so above; as above, so below.
[9.1] And how does a mendicant meditate as by day, so by night; as by night, so by day?
[9.2] It’s when a mendicant develops the basis of psychic power that has immersion due to enthusiasm, and active effort, with the same features, attributes, and signs by day as by night.
[9.3] And they develop it with the same features, attributes, and signs by night as by day.
[9.4] That’s how a mendicant meditates as by day, so by night; as by night, so by day.
[10.1] And how, with an open and unenveloped heart, does a mendicant develop a mind that’s full of radiance?
[10.2] It’s when a mendicant has properly grasped the perception of light, and has properly grasped the perception of day.
[10.3] That’s how, with an open and unenveloped heart, a mendicant develops a mind that’s full of radiance.
[11.1] And what is energy that’s too lax? …
[16.1] And what is mental development that’s too lax? …
[20.1] And what is inquiry that’s too lax?
[20.2] It’s when inquiry is coupled with laziness.
[20.3] This is called lax inquiry.
[21.1] And what is inquiry that’s too tense?
[21.2] It’s when inquiry is coupled with restlessness.
[21.3] This is called tense inquiry.
[22.1] And what is inquiry that’s constricted internally?
[22.2] It’s when inquiry is coupled with dullness and drowsiness.
[22.3] This is called inquiry constricted internally.
[23.1] And what is inquiry that’s scattered externally?
[23.2] It’s when inquiry is frequently scattered and diffused externally on account of the five kinds of sensual stimulation.
[23.3] This is called inquiry scattered externally. …
[23.4] That’s how, with an open and unenveloped heart, a mendicant develops a mind that’s full of radiance.

[7.3] and mind without greed as ‘mind without greed’.
[7.4] They understand mind with hate …
[7.5] mind without hate …
[7.6] mind with delusion …
[7.7] mind without delusion …
[7.8] constricted mind …
[7.9] scattered mind …
[7.10] expansive mind …

[7.14] mind immersed in samādhi …
[7.15] mind not immersed in samādhi …
[7.16] freed mind …

This was how I felt pleasure and pain, and that was how my life ended. [9.1] When the four bases of psychic power have been developed and cultivated in this way, with clairvoyance that is purified and superhuman, they see sentient beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in a good place or a bad place. When their body broke up, after death, they were reborn in a good place, a heavenly realm.’ And so, with clairvoyance that is purified and superhuman, they see sentient beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in a good place or a bad place. [3.3] this futile man Udāyī is not committed to the higher mind.
[3.4] Ānanda, how many topics for recollection are there?”

[4.2] What five?
[4.3] Firstly, a mendicant, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unskillful qualities, enters and remains in the first absorption … second absorption … third absorption.
[4.4] When this topic of recollection is developed and cultivated in this way it leads to blissful meditation in this very life.
[5.1] Furthermore, a mendicant focuses on the perception of light, focusing on the perception of day regardless of whether it’s night or day.
[5.2] And so, with an open and unenveloped heart, they develop a mind that’s full of radiance.

[6.3] When this topic of recollection is developed and cultivated in this way it leads to giving up sensual desire.
[7.1] Furthermore, suppose a mendicant were to see a corpse thrown in a charnel ground. And it had been dead for one, two, or three days, bloated, livid, and festering.
[7.2] They’d compare it with their own body:
[7.3] ‘This body is also of that same nature, that same kind, and cannot go beyond that.’

[8.2] They’d compare it with their own body:
[8.3] ‘This body is also of that same nature, that same kind, and cannot go beyond that.’

[9.2] A skeleton without flesh but smeared with blood, and held together by sinews …

[9.5] They’d compare it with their own body:

[9.7] When this topic of recollection is developed and cultivated in this way it leads to uprooting the conceit ‘I am’.
[10.1] Furthermore, giving up pleasure and pain, and ending former happiness and sadness, a mendicant enters and remains in the fourth absorption, without pleasure or pain, with pure equanimity and mindfulness.
[10.2] When this topic of recollection is developed and cultivated in this way it leads to the penetration of many elements.
[10.3] These are the five topics for recollection.”
[11.1] “Good, good, Ānanda.
[11.2] Well then, Ānanda, you should also remember this sixth topic for recollection.
[11.3] In this case, a mendicant goes out mindfully, returns mindfully, stands mindfully, sits mindfully, lies down mindfully, and focuses on work mindfully.

===== AN 8.64 Gayasisasutta (At Gaya Head) =====

[0.2] 7. Earthquakes
[0.3] At Gayā Head
[1.1] At one time the Buddha was staying near Gayā on Gayā Head.
[1.2] There the Buddha addressed the mendicants:
