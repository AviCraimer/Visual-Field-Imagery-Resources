Search agent's summary (unverified): A survey of scrying by an anthropologist who says he has never seen anything himself, with a long introduction by Andrew Lang reporting what his own acquaintances saw. Lang says the usual sequence in successful first attempts was that the glass ball 'grew milky, then black; then the pictures appeared', and that some strong visualisers got the mist but never pictures. Chapter III describes a first experiment made by looking into a perfectly dark funnel with the head covered, which 'filled with light' before pictures came. Chapter IV collects practical advice: sit alone, back to the light, ball on a dark cloth, exclude reflections, gaze about five minutes (Lang) or ten minutes or until the eyes tire, in repeated sets some days apart (Thomas); Lang holds that if nothing comes in two or three trials it probably never will. Historical chapters quote older recipes (Trithemius, Hollenzwang, Egyptian ink-mirror).

in  them  lies.  -  It  must  not  be  forgotten  that  sug-
gestion is  a  very  powerful  factor;  and  any  person
present  who  recognises  the  scene  or  persons  of  the
vision  should  give  no  hint  of  it,  much  less  ask  lead-
ing questions.  It  sometimes  happens  that  the
scryer  is  too  absorbed  in  the  vision,  and  needs
prompting.  This  should  be  done  by  someone  who
has  neither  part  nor  lot  in  the  scenes  represented.

Crystals  can  be  obtained  of  the  Society  for
Psychical  Research,  20  Hanover  Square,  London,
W.,  to  whose  care  reports  of  crystal  gazing  may  be
consigned  to  me.

I  have  sometimes  heard  it  suggested  that  crystal
gazing  is  injurious.  I  can  only  say  that  some,  of
the  most  normal  persons  I  know  are  excellent
crystal  gazers,  and  moderate  indulgence  in  the  sport
is  no  more  harmful  than  an  after-dinner  snooze.
