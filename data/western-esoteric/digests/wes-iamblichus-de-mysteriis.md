Search agent's summary (unverified): Porphyry asks about diviners who, remaining conscious, are inspired 'according to the phantastic part': some taking darkness as a co-operator, some potions, some chants and compositions, some working through water, some on a wall, some in the open air, some with sun or moon. Iamblichus answers that all these are one kind, the 'bringing of light' (photagogia): a divine light illuminates the soul's aether-like luminous vehicle and so moves the imagination. He lists the means: darkness, sunlight or moonlight, the open air, chants, conducting the light through water, or making it shine on a place in a wall prepared with sacred characters and fixed so it is not diffused. He insists the seer stays awake and attentive, and that the light comes from the gods' will, not the operator's technique. Book II grades the luminosity seen at epiphanies.
