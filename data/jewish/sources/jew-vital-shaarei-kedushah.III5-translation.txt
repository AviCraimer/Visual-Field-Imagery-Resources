# Sha'arei Kedushah, Part Three, Gate Five: "On the Nature of Prophecy: What It Is"

R. Ḥayyim Vital. Translated from the Hebrew text on Sefaria ("Shaarei Kedusha"). The paragraph numbers (§) follow Sefaria's segments.

---

**§2. The tree of souls.** It has been explained that there is one light, in the form of a human being (*adam*), which spreads through all four worlds:
- *Atzilut* (Emanation);
- *Beri'ah* (Creation);
- *Yetzirah* (Formation);
- *Asiyah* (Action).

It reaches down to the end of the four elements of the lower world. This light cleaves to the lights of the supernal Adam, which is called the ten *sefirot*, and they clothe themselves in this light, which is called "the light of the quarry of souls." All the souls of the lower beings are included there.

When the souls descend into this world to clothe themselves in bodies, they leave their roots cleaving to the source from which they were hewn. Only the branches of those roots go down, by way of extension alone, and clothe themselves in bodies in this world. It is like the branches of a tree, which are joined to the trunk itself and rooted in it, and bend down until they touch the ground while still attached to the trunk.

When a person commits a sin that deserves *karet* ("cutting off"), that branch is cut off from the trunk of the tree. It remains separated from it and stands in this world like the spirit of a beast. This is the secret of "that soul shall be utterly cut off" (Numbers 15:31), and the secret of "for man is a tree of the field" (Deuteronomy 20:19).

**§3. The doubled names.** This is the secret of why the names of the righteous are written twice: "Abraham, Abraham" (Genesis 22:11), "Jacob, Jacob" (Genesis 46:2), "Moses, Moses" (Exodus 3:4). One of the two corresponds to the aspect of the root, which remains above, cleaving to the tree, and this is called a person's *mazal*. As our Rabbis of blessed memory said (Menaḥot 29b), Moses our teacher saw the *mazal* of Rabbi Akiva sitting and expounding, etc. For from it the flow (*shefa'*) trickles (*nozel*) down to the branch that descended and clothed itself in the body.

**§4. Infinitely many roots.** The root is very high, at the very summit of the world of *Atzilut*. The branch is very long and extends through all the worlds until its tip clothes itself in this body. At every level in every world it leaves a root, so that every soul has roots without end, one above another. By his deeds a person merits raising all of them. For all the levels of the world of *Asiyah* — the roots he left there — are together called one complete *nefesh* of *Asiyah*, and the same holds for the other levels.

**§5. What prophecy requires.** This explains what prophecy is. Suppose a person's matter is refined:
- without the filth of the evil inclination;
- not under the powers of the elemental soul at all;
- with no sin to his name that blemishes any of the roots of his soul.

When he prepares himself to cleave to his supernal root, he can cleave to it. Yet even though he is fit for this, he must strip his soul entirely and separate it from all material things. Then it can cleave to its spiritual root.

**§6. What "divestment" does not mean.** The divestment (*hitpashtut*) you find written in all the books on prophecy and the Holy Spirit is not a literal divestment, in which the soul actually leaves the body as it does in sleep. If it were, that would not be prophecy but a dream, like any other dream. The resting of the Holy Spirit on a person happens while the soul is in his body, awake, and it does not leave him.

**§7. What divestment does mean.** The divestment is this:
- He removes all his thoughts completely.
- He stops his imaginative faculty (*koaḥ ha-medammeh*), a faculty that derives from the elemental animal soul in him, from imagining, thinking or musing about anything of the matters of this world, as though his soul had left him.
- Then the imaginative faculty turns its thoughts to imagining and picturing that he is ascending through the upper worlds, through the roots of his soul that are there, from one to the next, until the image in his imagination reaches its supernal source.
- The images of all the lights are engraved in his thought as though he were picturing and seeing them. This works the way the imaginative faculty pictures the things of this world in the mind even when he does not see them, as is known from natural philosophy.

**§8. Drawing down the light.** Then he should think and direct his intention to receive light from the ten *sefirot*, from the very point where the root of his soul is attached.
- He should intend to raise the ten *sefirot* as well, one to another, up to *Ein Sof* (the Infinite).
- He should then draw illumination down to them from there, descending all the way to their end.
- When the light comes down to them through him, they rejoice in it, and from that light they illuminate the root of his soul that is attached to them, according to the portion fitting for it.

**§9. Down to the imagination.** He should intend to bring it down, step by step, until that light and flow reach the intellective soul in his body. From there it reaches the animal soul and the imaginative faculty in it. There those matters take on a material image in his imaginative faculty, and then he understands them as though he were seeing them with his actual eyes.

**§10. Inner and outer senses.** Sometimes the light of the descending light is pictured in the imaginative faculty as the likeness of an angel speaking with him: he sees it, or hears its voice, or the like, through one of the five known inner (psychic) senses that belong to the imaginative faculty. From there the images may be transferred outward, to the five outer senses, which also belong to the animal soul, as is known. Then he sees, hears, smells and speaks with his actual physical senses, as it is written, "The spirit of the Lord spoke through me, and His word was on my tongue" (2 Samuel 23:2), for that light takes bodily form and image in the physical senses.

At other times his prophecy takes place only in the five spiritual senses within the imaginative faculty. All of this happens when the imaginative faculty is completely divested of every material thought, as said above.

**§11. How prophecy resembles a dream.** Prophecy is thus like a dream:
- the intellective soul goes out from him and rises upward, level by level;
- there it gazes and looks;
- afterwards it comes back down and channels this light down to the animal soul, where the imaginative faculty is;
- there those things take on image and more material form;
- when the person wakes, his soul remembers those matters by means of the retentive faculty and the faculty of memory, which also belong to the animal soul, as is known from natural philosophy.

**§12. Prophecy and dream distinguished.** So the nature of prophecy and the nature of the dream have been explained well: the one happens while the soul is in the body, the other after the soul has gone out. Prophecy itself, however, is of two kinds.

**§13. The first kind: the prophecy of the other prophets.** When the light flows down and reaches, in its descent, the intellective soul within him, and from there descends to the animal soul to be pictured in the five inner senses of the imaginative faculty, the outer senses would recoil in great trembling and the prophet would fall down. They had no strength to receive that light, picture it, and give it bodily form in the material senses. Prophecy of this kind is called a "dream," even though it is not actually a dream, as it is said, "and a deep sleep fell upon Abram" (Genesis 15:12).

**§14. The second kind: complete prophecy.** In complete prophecy the material senses did not recoil at all, and the images formed in them calmly. This was the prophecy of Moses our teacher, peace be upon him. The reason is that his matter was completely refined: through the holiness of his deeds it had been transformed to the level of soul. Its filth had been removed, and what remained was the good and refined element in his body, and also in the powers of his elemental soul. Only these have a material aspect; the intellective soul does not, nor anything above it.

**§15. The degrees of prophets.** It has already been explained that a person's soul has roots without end. According to the rank of the source it was hewn from, so great is its power to draw down the flow of prophecy.

Even if the root his soul was hewn from is in a high place, if he has not repaired all the roots from there downward and merited to draw through them, he cannot draw down the flow of his prophecy except from the level he has repaired and merited to reach. From there alone will the flow of his prophecy be drawn. This explains how there are endless degrees among the prophets.

**§16. The objection: how can mere thought ascend?** Do not think: "Since we have explained that the prophet's soul does not leave his body, what hold does his thought have — something insubstantial — to ascend and open the gates?" and so on.

You will understand this from what I wrote above. The soul is like a very long branch, extending from the root that cleaves to the tree down to the person's body. Along that line, which is always drawn out, whenever a person yearns to ascend to his root, the light of his thought rises. This light is called the *muskal* (the thing understood). It rises from the *maskil* (the one who understands), which is the intellective soul, up to the *sekhel* (the intellect) itself, which is the supernal root of his soul.

They cleave together and become one through the act of understanding itself, which is the flow drawn from the intellect to the one who understands. So the thing understood is the very light and flow that descends from the intellect to the intellective soul, and this flow and real light is what is called "thought."

Understand this well, for it is no empty matter. Otherwise all the matters of intention in prayer, and all a person's thoughts, good and bad, would be meaningless.

**§17. The branch and the tree.** So it has been explained to you that prophecy is necessary and possible. It is like a person who takes hold of the tip of a branch of a tree while it is bent down and shakes it with force: then the whole tree necessarily shakes.

Yet the upper beings are not moved by the thought of the lower person unless he is fit to draw the supernal light down upon them. Then, in this joy, they too draw some of that light down upon him. But if he does not have the ability to draw supernal light down upon them, they will not regard him at all. They will not consent to come near him to help him and to draw his thought upward, for it is vanity and there is no profit in it.
