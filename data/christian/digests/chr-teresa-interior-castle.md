Search agent's summary (unverified): Teresa's mature treatise for her nuns, written as instruction. Sixth Mansions chapters VIII to X go over the same ground as the Life but as teaching: the intellectual vision in which nothing is seen; the imaginative vision, which comes without warning, lasts about as long as a lightning flash, shows a living figure in 'an infused light like that of the sun covered with a veil as transparent as a diamond', and usually brings on ecstasy. She says the image is seen 'by the interior sight alone' and that she cannot speak of bodily apparitions because she never had one. She describes people of weak imagination who think they see what they picture, gives marks for telling the difference, and sets out reasons never to ask for or desire such visions. An editorial note states the scholastic threefold classification.
