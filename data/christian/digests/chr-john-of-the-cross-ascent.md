Search agent's summary (unverified): John's systematic treatise on the 'active night'. Book II, chapter X classifies supernatural apprehensions: those entering through the exterior bodily senses, those through the interior bodily sense (imagination), and purely spiritual ones. Chapter XI treats the first kind: spiritual persons sometimes see figures of saints or angels 'or certain extraordinary lights and brightness', hear words, smell perfumes. His instruction is flat: never rely on them or encourage them, 'fly from them, without examining whether they be good or evil', because the more exterior a thing is the less certain its origin and the more room for delusion; he lists the harms of accepting them. Later chapters apply the same rule to imaginative visions and describe spiritual visions as like a lightning flash that shows things in the dark.
