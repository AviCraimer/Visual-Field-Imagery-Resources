Search agent's summary (unverified): A manual for a young contemplative on the 'work' of lifting a naked intent to God in a 'cloud of unknowing', using a one-syllable word. Its relevance is negative and precise. The author says the darkness he means is not something pictured before the eyes, and equally that one should not imagine 'a clear shining light' in the dark. In chapter 52 he describes practitioners who misunderstand 'inward' work: they turn their bodily senses inward against nature and strain to see inwardly 'with their bodily eyes', overwork the imagination until they 'turn their brain in their heads', and then get 'false light or sounds', sweet smells, and 'quaint heats and burnings' in breast, back and bowels, while believing they are recollected. Later chapters describe people who stare upward and see lights.
