Search agent's summary (unverified): Teresa's autobiography, written under obedience for her confessors. Chapters XXVII to XXIX give her first-hand account of three kinds of vision and are explicit about the channel of each. The first was 'intellectual': a presence at her side with nothing seen by body or soul. Then came 'imaginary' visions of Christ's hands, face and whole glorified body, which she insists she 'never saw with my bodily eyes ... but only with the eyes of the soul', yet with a whiteness and infused brilliancy beside which sunlight looks dim, appearing instantaneously, and 'whether our eyes be open or shut, it makes no difference'. She says it cannot be reached 'by any diligence or efforts of our own', describes trying and failing to see more, and reports her confessors' fears, tests and her criteria for distinguishing true vision from imagination and from diabolical imitation.
