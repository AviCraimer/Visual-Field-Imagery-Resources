- J. P. Brown, The Dervishes, or Oriental Spiritualism (1868), archive.org dervishesororien00brow: seven lights/colours only as emblems of dress and Names; no practice-linked report of seen light (metaphorical_light).
- T. P. Hughes, A Dictionary of Islam (1885), art. 'Zikr' (archive.org dictionaryofisla00hugh): detailed zikr mechanics with closed eyes and held breath after Shah Wali Allah's Qawl al-jamil, but no statement about seen light (no_practical_content for this topic).
- E. H. Whinfield, Gulshan i Raz (1880) with notes from Lahiji (archive.org gulshanirazmysti00shabuoft): 'black light' couplets and glosses are doctrinal; Lahiji's own vision of black light is not carried here (metaphorical_light). See Corbin, Man of Light, ch. V for the vision.
- H. Wilberforce Clarke, The 'Awarifu-l-Ma'arif (1891) (archive.org UmarSuhrawardiAwarifAlMaarif): khilwat rules and the theory of waki'a (visions in dhikr), but concerns forms and dreams, no light phenomena (off_topic for seen light).
- Hazrat Inayat Khan, A Sufi Message of Spiritual Liberty (1914), The Way of Illumination (1924), The Soul Whence and Whither (1923) on archive.org: light is doctrinal or metaphorical throughout; practices (Zikar, Fikar, Kasab, Shagal, Amal) are only named (metaphorical_light).
