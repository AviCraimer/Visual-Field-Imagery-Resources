PREFACE. 

This is a small pamphlet in Persian called Risala-i-Haq-numa (haq 
— truth ; numa — pointing ; as a compass points out the North, so this points 
out the truth). It is from the pen of that enlightened prince Dara-Shikoh, 
tlio oldest son of Shah-i-Jahan, who died in 1659 A.l). In this book the 
author describes the four planes of existence, namely, the physical (Nisut), 
the super-physical (Malakut), the Blissful (Jabarut) and the Divine (Lahut), 
corresponding respectively with the four states of human consciousness, 
known as waking, dreaming, deep sleep and the trance-consciousness. The 
practices taught herein, are asserted by the royal author to have their 
origin in the holy Prophet himself, and since they were revealed to some 
few and well chosen disciples, and not preached publicly, therefore, they 
are not so well known in the exoteric faith of Islam. 

This translation, or rather an adaptation, is the work of one of the 
members of the Islamia Lodge of the Theosophical Society, Benares. The 
leai ned President of this Lodge, Moulvi Muhammad Umar, explained the 
pamphlet in the I.odge meetings, and this English rendering is the result 
of these teachings given by tlie learned and spiritual President of the 
branch.

There are two Paths that lead to that Lord of Holiness. 

THE PATH OP GRACE. 

The first is the Path of Grace, and the second that of Exertion. It is 
grace when the Lord takes the seeker to a Master, and that Perfect Man, 
by one glance at the disciple, accomplishes all liis work : removes the
