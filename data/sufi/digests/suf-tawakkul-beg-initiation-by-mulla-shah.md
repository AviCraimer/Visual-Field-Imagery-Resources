Search agent's summary (unverified): First-person narrative of a 17th-century Qadiri novice. After reciting sura 112 a hundred thousand times he sits whole nights facing Mecca, concentrating on his own heart while the master and two adepts direct their attention (tawajjuh) at him. Three nights nothing; on the fourth, towards dawn, 'some little light and clearness' appear in the heart without colour or form. The master then bandages his eyes, tells him to reproduce the master's image and concentrate on the heart; the heart 'opens', he sees a replica of the cell and the master with eyes bound, then a dazzling figure, then over three months processions of figures, and finally 'the sphere where all color is effaced'. Macdonald adds a paragraph on Sufi schemes of seven coloured lights and the teachers who warned that such lights belong to the body.
