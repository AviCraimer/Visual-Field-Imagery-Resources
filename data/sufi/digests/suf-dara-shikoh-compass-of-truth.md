Search agent's summary (unverified): A short practical manual in four 'planes' (Nasut, Malakut, Jabarut, Lahut) by the Mughal prince, written for seekers without a teacher. It tells the practitioner to sit alone, close the eyes, fix attention on the heart and see the form of the Master there; says the form seen after closing the eyes belongs to the 'alam-i mithal and that with practice the mirror of the heart brightens and forms of prophets and saints appear. It then gives a detailed breath-retention exercise with the fingers sealing ears, eyes, lips and nostrils (eyes shut by the index fingers 'but the fingers should not press the eye-balls'), and reports heat, buoyancy and 'a subtle illumination in thy heart, and in all thy body'. The Sultan-ul-azkar section concerns inner sound more than light. The translator's theosophical notes are interleaved.
