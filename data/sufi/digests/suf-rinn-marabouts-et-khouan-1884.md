Search agent's summary (unverified): A French colonial survey of Algerian Sufi orders that prints long translated extracts from the orders' own manuals. The Khalwati chapter gives, after al-Sanusi, the seated posture and the head movement of the la ilaha illa'llah dhikr, the series of Names taken up one at a time on the shaykh's word, and a description of what the adept sees: apparitions come only in solitude after long devotions; first the light of ablutions and prayers, then a demonic light, then forms; then seventy thousand lights distributed over seven degrees, each with its colour and a condition for seeing it (for the red lights, food must be stripped of what one most craves, or they appear dulled by smoke, in which case one should go no further). It also quotes Sha'rani's criticism that such visions are products of exhaustion.

néant.  Cette  lumière,  qui  découle  d’un  seul  nom,  ne  s’étend  pas  sur
